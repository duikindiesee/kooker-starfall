## Proposed finite world

Use a new explicit world identity, `starfall.coastal-world.v1`, with immutable seed, dimensions, content revision and source fingerprint. Initial design envelope: 1,200 x 1,600 metres, with a walkable shore route of at least 600 metres and layered mesa walls roughly 80–160 metres above the river. These are design targets, not measured implementation facts. Retain the tree at its established physical scale; do not enlarge everything uniformly. Connect the rocky tree bank, open sand plain, canyon passage, river mouth, sea overlook and submerged shelf as one landscape. Boundaries must be legible and physically enforced. Distant decorative geometry must be labelled separately from reachable land.

## Canyon, river and signature vista

The user's latest direction uses Namibia's Fish River Canyon as spatial inspiration: broad, layered arid canyon walls and a convincing long river corridor. This is an art-direction instruction, not a surveyed reconstruction or a claim that geographic reference research has been completed. Starfall's canyon is alive with luminous light-turquoise freshwater. A continuous meandering river widens naturally into deeper pools and basins, forms rocky banks and crossings, then visibly opens into a larger, darker sea. Preserve broad valley space, irregular layered slopes and distant walls; a cramped trench, repeated cliff slabs, isolated ponds or uniform water colour fails the composition gate.

The terrain uses a flat coordinate system with streamable terrain at the systems level. Horizon composition, distant land and sky establish the planetary impression; no spherical-world implementation is implied. Preserve connected gameplay routes and intentional unreachable-island progression. Each island needs an explicit initial reachability state and an intended later traversal capability or route; inspection flight does not count as unlocking it.

From elevated canyon viewpoints and along the canyon axis, the turquoise river must lead the eye toward its ocean opening. Frame the blue giant low beyond that opening, accompanied by several smaller, clearly separated moons. Their relative scale, depth/parallax and very slow motion/orbits must read as coherent celestial bodies. Do not exaggerate parallax merely to make motion obvious, or use a flat decal cluster or visually colliding discs. Preserve the composition across appropriate weather/time states, documenting deliberate cloud occlusion and visibility limits. Ground-level navigation and performance remain acceptance constraints. These requirements apply to the integrated candidate as well as the coastal main world; they do not establish implemented landscape or celestial behaviour.

## Starting watershed and progression

The inhabitant begins naturally confined within the canyon watershed. High canyon walls, steep slopes, deep water and the ocean provide believable physical boundaries; invisible walls do not satisfy this starting-region design. The enclosure must still offer substantial exploration through riverbanks, connected pools, caves or overhangs, crossings, terraces and resource pockets. Upper plateaus and offshore islands remain visible future destinations, with later climbing routes and boat travel able to unlock them. Do not make every visible destination immediately reachable or treat deep water as a route before the required travel capability exists.

Physical world bounds remain a systems contract and defensive fallback, not the player-facing enclosure. Terrain and water hazards must prevent ordinary movement from reaching an exposed technical edge. Test both the player and the existing inhabitant's navigation when integrated: neither may walk through cliffs, become trapped in collider seams or confined spaces, or fall out of the world. Preserve deliberate progression barriers while providing usable return routes from all currently reachable locations. Caves/overhangs require actual supporting and blocking geometry beyond a simple heightfield.

## Canyon-rim milestone

The canyon rim and elevated panoramic vista are difficult but achievable destinations, not permanently inaccessible scenery. Most sheer walls remain unclimbable early. Provide one discoverable staged ascent using readable ledges and a narrow switchback, or a later simple climbing-gear/stamina capability. Specify the chosen route and prerequisites explicitly rather than silently requiring all possible mechanics. Include safe rest points, a usable return route and recovery from falls. Invisible barriers and arbitrary jump puzzles do not satisfy this progression.

Reaching the rim is a meaningful milestone revealing the canyon river, ocean, distant islands, blue giant and separated moons together in an actual player panoramic view. Preserve ground-level composition and navigation while framing this elevated reward. Inspection flight or teleportation cannot establish achievement of the ascent.

Before the inhabitant has discovered the route and acquired its required climbing capability, navigation and action validation must reject unsafe ascent attempts. Do not rely solely on model restraint or a visually steep surface. Test route knowledge and capability restrictions independently, then verify eligible traversal after unlock. The rim exception refines the starting watershed enclosure: early confinement remains physical and believable, with one intentional progression route rather than every cliff becoming climbable.

## Starting refuge

A small natural cave or deep rock overhang must be visible or discoverable near the initial spawn. It is a primitive refuge, not a finished house. Place its dry navigable floor above the documented maximum starting-region flood and wave envelope, with room for later fire, storage, bedding and construction upgrades. Those upgrades remain future capabilities.

The refuge must attenuate wind and rain and yield warmer perceived conditions through the shared environment model. Test equivalent sheltered and exposed positions under the same weather state; a roof silhouette alone does not establish shielding. Keep the entrance readable in relevant lighting/weather states. Provide enough collision and camera clearance for the inhabitant to stand, sit, sleep and carry items without clipping, including transitions between poses and movement through the entrance. All reachable interior positions need a usable exit path; neither player nor NPC may become trapped.

Reserve stable location identity `starting-refuge` under the versioned world definition. Once integrated, actual perception should discover it and memory should identify it as the first safe place using verified shelter/navigation observations. Do not prepopulate a claimed discovery, invent a memory event contract, or bypass the existing NPC executor and memory provenance checks. Fire safety, item storage and construction semantics require their own later implementation.

## Inhabitant boundary

Future location IDs: `tree-bank`, `canyon-gate`, `river-mouth`, `sea-overlook`, `submerged-shelf`. Objects require stable IDs and explicit capability/activity metadata; proximity does not grant permission. Navigation must derive from the same collision surfaces used by the player. Reachability and discoverability must be measured, including unavailable underwater capabilities. World revisions must invalidate stale navigation and observations explicitly. The existing NPC executor remains the sole action authority, and memory provenance remains owned by its existing workstream. This branch does not connect a model, publish memory events, or introduce an alternative executor.

## Environment integration contract

The separate environment workstream's `Assets/CityLife/Environment/EnvironmentModel.cs` was read on 13 September. `Starfall.EnvironmentFoundation.IEnvironmentSurface` supplies `WorldId`, `Revision`, `PhysicalBounds`, `Contains`, `TryGround` (height and normal), `WaterLevel`, `WaterDepth`, and `Current`. WaterDepth is bed-to-surface depth; signed immersion is `WaterLevel(position) - position.y` only where water depth is positive. The component adapter currently returns zero current and samples the analytic coastal terrain; this is not an implemented flowing river or an exact rendered-triangle contact test. Player body motion must still use actual collision geometry.

The environment clock uses 0.02-second ticks. Main-world integration must supply a new adapter for the new physical bounds and world revision, validate all route samples against rendered/collision terrain, and consume the shared weather/water contract without copying its simulation into this branch. The adapter has been inspected as source only here; the environment owner's reported tests and pending compiled-player checks are separate evidence. No environment source has been imported, no runtime has been connected, and no Unity process is controlled by this documentation change.
